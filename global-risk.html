A reproducible calculation, with editorial limits

Each category receives an editorial severity level from 0 to 5: 0 minimal, 1 limited, 2 moderate, 3 high, 4 severe, 5 exceptional. Levels reflect the assessed pressure in that domain, not equal statistical probabilities. Climate can represent prospective pressure; health can represent concentrated regional pressure.

Conflict and civilian protection

4/5 × 25% weight → 20 points

Economy and energy

4/5 × 25% weight → 20 points

Public health and cross-border readiness

3/5 × 25% weight → 15 points

Climate outlook and preparedness

4/5 × 25% weight → 20 points

Calculation: (4 + 4 + 3 + 4) ÷ 20 × 100 = 75. All four weights are 25%. Equal weighting is a publishing choice, not a claim that the domains have identical consequences. This method does not estimate event probabilities or the likelihood of personal harm. Sources support the underlying signals; the scores are Pulse Of Globe’s interpretation.

How to read this report

Events and forecasts

An observed event, a surveillance count and an economic or climate projection have different meanings. Forecast periods and observation dates stay attached to the underlying sources.

Coverage and uncertainty

This is a selected global overview, not an exhaustive emergency inventory. Case totals can be revised. Markets can move after publication. A source checked today does not necessarily contain today’s observations.

Editorial assistance

AI assisted the organization and writing of this review. Scores are transparent editorial judgments rather than institutional forecasts. No automatic daily news feed or automatic score updating is implied.

Global Risk Index questions

Is 75/100 a 75% chance of a disaster?

No. It is a scaled editorial severity indicator, not a probability.

Why is this lower than the old 84/100?

The method changed. The two values cannot establish an improvement or deterioration.

Does the report update automatically every day?

No. It is a dated review. New reporting requires a new source check and a coordinated update of content, metadata and the data file.

Are the climate and economic figures observed outcomes?

No. The cited figures are projections or outlooks for the periods stated in their sources.
