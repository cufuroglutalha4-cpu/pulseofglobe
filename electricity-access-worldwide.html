How Many People Have No Electricity?

The 2025 Tracking SDG 7 report puts global electricity access at 92% in 2023, with 666 million people without access. These are dated access estimates, not a current outage count.

Access is different from reliability

An electricity-access indicator is not a measure of continuous supply, affordability or the number of people affected by a temporary blackout. A person can be counted as having access and still experience interruptions. Keep these questions separate when interpreting a headline.

What the reference year means

The report was published in 2025 using a 2023 snapshot. We retain both dates so readers do not mistake publication for observation. Counts and percentages are rounded, so deriving a world population total by dividing one rounded figure by another introduces avoidable error.

Comparing energy indicators

Installed power capacity measures potential output, electricity consumption measures energy used, and access concerns service availability. They have different units and denominators. Use this page for access context and the linked consumption page for the scale of energy use.

Frequently asked questions

Does 666 million count temporary blackouts?

No. It describes the reported electricity-access gap.

Why does a 2025 report show 2023 data?

Global monitoring is published after data collection and consolidation; the reference and publication years differ.
