World Urban Population

UN World Urbanization Prospects 2025 reports that about 45% of the world’s population lives in cities. Its harmonized approach distinguishes cities, towns and rural areas.

Why 45% is not the same as every urban statistic

A headline about cities does not necessarily include towns and other settlements called urban in national statistics. The 2025 revision uses a harmonized settlement classification. Do not combine percentages based on that classification with a national-definition series without checking their coverage.

What counts as a megacity?

The report counts 33 megacities with at least ten million residents in 2025. That threshold describes settlement size rather than an administrative title. A city-proper boundary, a metropolitan area and a harmonized urban centre can produce different totals for the same place.

A simple way to compare sources

Before ranking places, write down the boundary definition, reference year and dataset revision. If any of those differ, present the values separately. This page intentionally avoids attaching a live counter to a periodically published settlement estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Does this mean only 45% live in urban areas?

Not under every definition. The city category is narrower than some uses of urban.

Why do city rankings differ?

Boundary definitions and reference years can differ, even for the same named city.
