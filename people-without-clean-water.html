How Many People Lack Safe Drinking Water?

WHO and UNICEF reported that 2.1 billion people lacked safely managed drinking-water services in 2024. Their release was published in August 2025; this is not a live 2026 headcount.

Safe water is a defined service level

Safely managed drinking water refers to an improved source that is available when needed, accessible on premises and free from contamination. Someone below that service level is not necessarily using surface water; these are different categories.

Why definitions matter more than a ticking clock

A household’s service level is not measured every second worldwide. A smoothly changing display would suggest information the underlying survey and monitoring process cannot provide. This page therefore shows a dated snapshot instead of inventing a daily access counter.

How to read an access headline

Check whether the figure describes people, households or a percentage. Then check the service category and data year. When comparing two reports, use matching definitions and consider revisions. The worldwide count does not describe the water quality of any individual tap.

Frequently asked questions

Are all 2.1 billion drinking surface water?

No. Surface-water users are a smaller, distinct group within the broader service gap.

Is this a water-consumption counter?

No. Access to a service and litres consumed are different indicators.
