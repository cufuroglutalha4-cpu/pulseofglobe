World Population Milestones: From 1 to 8 Billion

The United Nations marked 15 November 2022 as the day the world reached 8 billion people. Milestones describe estimated thresholds, not a synchronized global census.

Why milestone dates are estimates

A milestone year condenses a complicated measurement problem into a memorable reference point. Birth registration, death registration and censuses are collected on different schedules. Historical reconstructions are especially uncertain, so a date should not be read as identifying an exact individual.

History and projections belong in different columns

The table below uses the historical sequence in the UN’s 1999 milestone document and the later 2022 announcement for eight billion. We do not reuse the old document’s future population forecasts. A projection is conditional on assumptions and can change when a new population revision is published.

How to use the table

Compare the gaps between milestones rather than subtracting rounded totals to infer an exact growth rate. The interval from 1804 to 1927 is 123 years, while 1927 to 1960 is 33 years. Those arithmetic comparisons describe the listed milestones only. For a current modelled counter, use World Population Live.

Frequently asked questions

Are these exact census dates?

No. They are demographic estimates and commemorative milestones.

Can a milestone year change?

Yes. Revisions can alter historical estimates and the dates implied by them.
