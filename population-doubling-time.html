The exact doubling-time formula

With annual compounding, P(t) = P(0) × (1 + r/100)^t. Setting P(t) to twice its starting value gives t = ln(2) / ln(1 + r/100). The initial population cancels out, so the calculator needs only the percentage growth rate.

What the rule of 70 does

The shortcut divides 70 by the annual percentage rate. At 1%, it gives 70 years; at 2%, it gives 35 years. Compare these with the exact outputs to see the approximation error. Use the exact formula for calculations where the distinction matters.

Why this is not a prediction

The tool holds the rate fixed indefinitely. It does not model changing fertility, mortality, age structure or migration. Zero growth never doubles; negative growth shrinks rather than doubles. A future population projection requires a demographic model, not just this formula.

Frequently asked questions

What happens at zero growth?

There is no finite doubling time.

Does the starting population matter?

No. For a fixed compound growth rate, the ratio doubles over the same interval.
