Ageing World Population

The UN projects that the number of people aged 65 and over could reach 2.2 billion by the late 2070s, exceeding the number of children under 18. This is a long-range projection.

Always check the age threshold

A statistic about people aged 60 and over cannot be substituted for a statistic about those aged 65 and over. The same applies to children under 15 versus children under 18. This page keeps the thresholds in the UN comparison visible alongside the headline.

A count and a share answer different questions

An older population can grow in number even when its share changes slowly. A share needs both an age-group count and the total population for the same place, year and dataset. We do not divide the long-range projection above by today’s world population counter.

Using a projection responsibly

The reference is useful for discussing future care, housing and accessibility needs. It is not a precise schedule for an individual country. When making comparisons, record the projection edition, age threshold, reference year and scenario before calculating a percentage or growth rate.

Frequently asked questions

Is ageing the same as population decline?

No. Age composition and total population size are different measurements.

Is the 2.2-billion figure observed?

No. It is a projected future count, not a present-day total.
