World Fertility Rate

UN World Population Prospects 2024 estimated global fertility at 2.25 live births per woman, compared with 3.31 in 1990. These are reference-year estimates, not live 2026 measurements.

Fertility rate is not births per second

Total fertility rate summarizes a set of age-specific fertility rates. It is a different kind of measure from a count of births in one day or a crude birth rate per thousand residents. Compare indicators only after checking the unit and reference year.

What the comparison tells us

The two quoted UN figures differ by 1.06 births per woman. Dividing that difference by 3.31 gives a decline of about 32.0%. This page calculates that percentage from the rounded published values; it does not imply equal declines in every country or every year.

Why a falling rate does not immediately mean fewer people

A population total also depends on how many people are in each age group, survival and migration. A fertility indicator alone cannot tell you today’s net population change. For that question, compare birth and death totals and consult the age structure used by the demographic model.

Frequently asked questions

Is 2.25 a live rate?

No. It is the reference estimate in the UN’s 2024 summary.

Is fertility the same as births per year?

No. Fertility is expressed in births per woman; annual births are a count of events.
